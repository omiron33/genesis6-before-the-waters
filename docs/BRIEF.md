# Visual notes

Before the Waters sets Genesis 6 in the words of the Septuagint ("Noe"): the mingling of the sons of God with the daughters of men, the giants of old, God's grief over a corrupt earth, Noe who found grace, the command to build the ark, the coming flood, the covenant, the gathering of every living kind, and Noe's obedience. The recording is alternative metal at about 95 BPM: distorted guitar riffs, dry live drums, an uneasy half-time pulse that grows urgent, drops nearly silent, surges into a heavy crescendo and ends quiet and resolute. A low female mezzo, close in the verses and belting at the peaks.

The film answers it with weight: an ancient world under a darkening sky, heavy stone and bronze, forge fire and storm light, and against all of it the clean gold of grace and the honest timber of the ark.

## Reading

The film follows the older reading held by many early Fathers (with the Book of Enoch, and the Septuagint manuscripts that read "angels of God"): the "sons of God" are fallen angels, the Watchers who came down on Mount Hermon, took wives from the daughters of men and taught them metalwork, weapons, ornaments and cosmetics. Their union created the Nephilim, the giants, the men of renown. The angels are never shown as figures: they are cold, too-bright lights that fall from the sky, beautiful but wrong, fire that does not warm. The Nephilim appear as colossal, distant, perfectly still dark silhouettes in the smoke, taller than the towers, rim-lit by forge glow with a faint cold edge from their fathers' light; never close, never moving, no faces. Their works (walls, weapons, monuments) carry the rest. God's grief and "repenting" are shown as weather and stillness, never as a face. Noe is just and obedient; grace is God's gift, shown as warm gold light. The ark is the Church: a lit refuge, its door in the side.

## The arc: dusk to the first rain

The whole film takes place in the last evening of the old world, from a bruised dusk to night and the first rain.

- **Opening and verse 1: the mountain and the valley.** Mount Hermon, high and dark at dusk; below, the valley of Cain, walled cities glowing with forges. Cold lights hang above the peak and look down; they fall like slow meteors onto the summit and their glow streams down the mountain into the red light of the valley and through its gate. The daughters of men are shown by their world, touched by the Watchers' cold light: bronze mirrors, gold ornaments, looms, lit windows. "My Spirit will not stay": the altar smoke on the mountain bends and is torn away by wind. "One hundred twenty years": a measure of time (notched stone, a sand-glass, rings of a felled cedar). "Giants": colossal works in the valley, a bronze sword taller than a tower, walls of cyclopean stone, a throne of basalt, a giant footprint in mud.
- **Breakdown (near silence): God sees.** The city from very high in black smoke; embers in the dark ("every heart brooding"); a vast cloud building, lit from within; stillness. "Cattle, creatures that crawl, birds of the sky": herds as distant silhouettes on a plain, tracks, a flock wheeling at dusk. "I grieve": the first heavy drops of rain on dust.
- **Bridge: grace.** "But Noe found grace": light breaks through the cloud onto one place, a homestead in a cedar grove. A straight cedar trunk and a plumb line ("a just man"); three young cedars or three lamps for Shem, Ham and Japheth.
- **Build: the corruption.** The valley at full night: crimson furnaces, smoke, roads choked; "the end of all men is before Me": a wall of storm on the horizon, lightning inside it.
- **Verse 2: the ark built.** Square timber, adze marks, wet black pitch brushed within and without; the ark's dimensions drawn in lines of light over the timber yard (300, 50, 30 cubits); the window a cubit above; the door in the side; three stories in a lit cutaway.
- **Climax: the flood announced.** The storm wall arrives: rain in sheets, the fountains of the deep, water rising across the plain.
- **Chorus: the covenant.** The ark from outside in the storm, lit warm from within; the open door full of gold light.
- **Verse 3: every living kind.** Pairs of tracks in the mud converging on the ramp; pairs of birds settling on the ark's roof; feathers; hoofprints; the hold stocked with grain jars, figs, hay.
- **Outro: obedience.** The finished ark on its hill in the first rain at night, the door lit, quiet; the end title.

## Look

Dark, heavy, cinematic, never murky: every frame has a clearly lit subject and real depth (lens focus, haze, soft shadows, rim light). Something moves at every moment: fire, smoke, cloud, rain, dust, the camera. The camera has more push than a ballad: purposeful dollies, cranes and rises timed to the riffs, cuts on the beat, the occasional run of fast cuts on the heaviest hits, and real holds in the near-silent drop.

Palette:
- storm violet and slate teal skies at dusk, bruised and heavy
- forge orange and furnace crimson for Cain's valley and the corruption
- cold star-white for the fallen angels' light; cold silver and moonlight for the mountain
- the gold of grace (warm, clean, never orange) for God's favour and the covenant
- honey cedar and glossy black pitch for the ark
- ash grey for judgment and the flood

No human figures. People appear at most as distant, still silhouettes or by what they leave behind (lamps carried, a mirror set down, tools on a bench, footprints). No faces, no moving people, no human shadows moving. God is the voice and the light, never a figure. The giants are never shown as creatures, only through their monuments and weapons.

## Words

The words are the song's, so they are a star: sharp, readable (4.5:1 against what is behind them) and set in a dark calm part of the frame, never over a bright sky or a fire.

- The narration: EB Garamond, upright, warm off-white.
- The words of God ("My Spirit will not stay", "I will wipe away", "The end of all men", "Make yourself an ark", "I will bring a flood", "I will establish My covenant"): EB Garamond italic in the gold of grace.
- The heavy hits (giants, wickedness, corrupt, wrong, flood, perish, destroy): tall Bebas Neue capitals in ash or forge red, sometimes very large, landing on the drum.
- Grace, just, perfect, covenant, Noe: gold, lit.
- Numbers and measures (one hundred twenty, three hundred, fifty, thirty cubits): set like a builder's measure, Bebas Neue with thin rules, in silver.
- Names (Shem, Ham, Japheth): spaced capitals.

Layouts vary with the picture and the meaning: position, size and grouping answer the composition. At most two lines at once.
